XR Auto Service

Hands-free repair guidance on a headset, and the knowledge graph that answers a technician’s questions about the car in front of them.

Role
Built it end to end, from the headset app to the retrieval behind its assistant
Stack
Unity (C#), Python, knowledge graphs, hybrid retrieval, Meta Quest 3

The problem

Cars change faster than a technician can memorize them, and the people who know every system are few. A technician working on something unfamiliar looks the procedure up on a screen across the bay, walks back, and tries to remember the torque spec on the way. The expertise exists, in service documentation, but not where the work happens.

What I built

XR Auto Service puts the procedure on a Meta Quest 3. The technician is walked through a repair one step at a time, with reference photos, safety warnings and a confirmation photo at each step, and can ask a question out loud about the step they are on. The app works offline and syncs its records when the headset reconnects.

The answers come from a knowledge graph I built over licensed service information, filtered to the exact vehicle on the lift, so a question about one engine never pulls the procedure for another.

Data the company may use

Before any of the retrieval, I built a toolkit that collects only service data the company is allowed to use, and records where every document came from and under what licence. That manifest is what makes the knowledge base usable in a product at all, and it means any answer can be traced back to its source.

How I know it works

Retrieval is scored automatically on every change, against questions that repair experts wrote with the procedure they expected, and a model judge grades the answers. When I tested the evaluation itself, it caught a broken ranker but could not separate several reasonable ones, so I use it as a guard against regressions rather than a leaderboard.
